Why shadow-running beats a big-bang go-live

By the Embodi Deployment Team · August 3, 2026 · 6 min read

The riskiest moment in any automation rollout isn't the install — it's the first hour a new system touches a real part with no human hands nearby. Most failed pilots we've inherited didn't fail because the perception model was bad. They failed because the first time it made a decision on live production, nobody had a way to catch a bad call before it shipped.

What shadow-running actually means

Once Embodi Vision and Task Agents are trained on your parts, we don't cut over the cell immediately. Instead, the system runs in shadow mode: it watches the same parts your operators or existing process are handling, makes its own pick, place, and inspection decisions, and logs them — without ever moving a gripper into the live path.

Every decision is scored against what actually happened, part by part, shift by shift. A plant engineer gets a daily readout: agreement rate, where the agent would have diverged from the human or PLC-driven process, and why.

Why this changes the internal conversation

Plant managers don't need to take our word that a system is ready. They can look at two weeks of shadow-run data on their own line, on their own parts, and decide for themselves. That's a fundamentally different sell than a vendor demo reel.

  • Shadow-run agreement rates above 97% are typically ready for a supervised go-live.
  • Divergences are usually clustered in a handful of edge-case part orientations — easy to target for additional training.
  • Because nothing touches the live path yet, there is zero line-down risk during validation.

What happens after go-live

Shadow-running doesn't stop once a cell goes live. Task Agents continue to log confidence scores on every action, and Fleet Orchestrator routes anything below threshold to a human operator instead of guessing. That's the same discipline that got the cell approved in the first place — it just keeps running.

Pose estimation for parts you've never labeled

By the Embodi Vision Team · July 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Classical pose-estimation pipelines want thousands of labeled images per SKU before they're reliable. On a line running dozens of SKUs with weekly supplier changes, that math never closes — by the time a dataset is labeled, the part has already changed.

Few-shot calibration instead of dataset collection

Embodi Vision separates "understanding geometry" from "understanding this specific part." The geometric backbone is pretrained across a broad library of industrial part shapes. When a new SKU shows up, calibration only needs a handful of reference captures — typically under fifteen minutes on the line — rather than a labeling project.

That reference pass captures the part from a small number of angles under your actual cell lighting, which matters more than volume: a model calibrated on your real conditions with a dozen images consistently outperforms one trained on thousands of images from a different lighting setup.

Handling tolerance stacking

Real parts aren't the CAD file. Stamped and cast components carry tolerance stacking that classical template matching struggles with. Embodi Vision's pose estimator outputs a confidence-weighted 6D pose rather than a single best guess, so Task Agents can decide whether to proceed, re-grip, or flag for inspection based on how confident the read actually is.

Where this shows up on the floor

  • New SKU onboarding measured in minutes, not weeks of dataset collection.
  • Fewer false-confident grasps on out-of-tolerance parts, because confidence is explicit rather than implicit.
  • Vision QA reuses the same pose and confidence signal to flag likely defects without a separate inspection model.

The result is a perception stack that keeps up with how often your supply chain actually changes — which, on most lines we've deployed to, is far more often than a dataset team could ever keep pace with.
